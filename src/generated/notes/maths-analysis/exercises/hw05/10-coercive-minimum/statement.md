Let $f(x)$ be continuous over $\mathbb{R}$ and satisfy the “coercive” condition:

$$
f(x)\ge a|x|-b,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},
$$

where $a>0$ and $b>0$ are two constants.

:::list

1.  **(a)** Show that $\inf f$ exists.

2.  **(b)** Show that $f$ attains $\inf f$ somewhere at $x_0\in\mathbb{R}$. That is, there is some $x_0$ such that

    $$
    f(x_0)=\inf f.
    $$

    Such a point $x_0$ is called a global minimum point of $f$ over $\mathbb{R}$.

:::

(Hint: Let $\{x_n\}$ be a sequence such that

$$
f(x_n)\to\inf f\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.
$$

Show that $\{x_n\}$ contains a convergent subsequence.)
