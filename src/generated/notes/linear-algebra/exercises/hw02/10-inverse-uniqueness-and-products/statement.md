Call $M\in\mathcal M_{m\times m}$ invertible if there is $N\in\mathcal M_{m\times m}$ with $MN=NM=I$. Prove that such an $N$ is unique (so $M^{-1}$ is well defined), and that if $M,N$ are invertible then so is $MN$, with $(MN)^{-1}=N^{-1}M^{-1}$.
