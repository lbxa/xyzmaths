# Introduction {id="ch:introduction-to-analysis" class="unnumbered"}

Analysis studies how infinite processes interact with arithmetic and geometry. A sequence may approach a value without reaching it, and a series may have a finite sum despite having infinitely many terms. Completeness explains why the limiting values required by these processes exist in the real numbers.

The distinction between existence and computation runs throughout this book. A proposed formula for a limit does not show that the sequence converges. A drawing may suggest that a curve reaches a value, while continuity and completeness provide the justification. We will repeatedly turn such suggestions into arguments by choosing a tolerance, bounding an error, and showing that the bound works wherever the conclusion requires it.

The foundations chapter introduces the set, order, and limit tools used later. The subsequent chapters develop sequences, series, continuity, and differentiation within the book’s stated assumptions. Some later sections remain outlines. Their headings preserve the intended progression, and a historical introduction does not replace a missing definition or proof.
