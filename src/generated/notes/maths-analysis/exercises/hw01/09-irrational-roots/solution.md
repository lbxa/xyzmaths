:::div{class="proof"}

*Solution.* Let $k\ge1$. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $a^{1/k}$ is rational. Then

$$
a^{1/k}=\frac pq,
  \qquad p,q\in\mathbb{Z},\quad q\ne0.
$$

Raising both sides to the $k$th power gives

$$
a=\left(\frac pq\right)^k=\frac{p^k}{q^k}\in\mathbb{Q},
$$

since $p^k,q^k\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $q^k\ne0$. This contradicts the assumption that $a$ is irrational. Therefore $a^{1/k}$ is irrational for every $k\in\mathbb{N}$. ◻
